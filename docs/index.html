How it plays

Every loop is a lantern

One verb, three beats. Learning when each creature pauses is the whole game.

1

Fly

Your moth drifts and flaps through the dark, leaving a trail of glowing dots that fades after a few seconds.

2

Loop

Cross your own trail around a creature and it wraps into a cocoon. Loop a whole ledge to catch whatever walks on it.

3

Collect

A firefly hatches from each cocoon. Catch it before it wakes, and when every firefly is free the cave lights up.

Co-op

Two moths, one light

Hand a friend the second controller. A loop made from both moths' trails counts: its fireflies are worth double and it drops a power-up in the middle. A caught moth becomes a spirit that the other can fly into to bring it back.

The cave

Six creatures, six ways to catch them

Each creature pauses at some point, because a loop takes a few seconds to fly. That pause is your moment.

Dust bunny

Walks its ledge. Loop the ledge.

Shadow bat

Flees your light, then flies home to doze. Loop it asleep.

Snuffer beetle

Eats your trail and breaks loops. Go wide, be quick.

Mimic stone

A rock until the light finds it. Then it chases.

Wisp

Harmless, but its touch steals your light for a while.

Moth-eater spider

Drops, hangs and spins webs that slow you down.

Every fifth cave

Three bosses in the deep

Bosses are drawn into the cave itself and light up like the walls do. You can't loop a boss, so loop what it sends out.

Cave 5 · 4 hits

The Great Owl

Loop its moon orb while it hovers. Feathers fall while the owl is awake.

Cave 10 · 6 hits

The Cave Toad

Loop its rising bubbles, two at once for two hits. Never fly over its mouth.

Cave 15 · 8 hits

The Snuffer Queen

Loop her embers while her larvae hatch on the ledges and eat your trail.

Under the hood

A real NES cartridge

Lantern Moth is written in 6502 assembly for the simplest NES board there is. It runs on original hardware from a flash cart, and on any NES emulator.

CartridgeNROM-256 (mapper 0): 32 KB of program, 8 KB of graphics, no extra chips
LightAll of it is colour attributes and palettes. Each 16×16 block is dark, dim, lit or flaring, and no tiles are spent on light.
LoopsFound by scanline parity over a 32×30 bit mask, spread over a few frames, with no flood fill and no stack
CavesPacked at 4 bits a cell with an overlay for boss art, 448 bytes each
Frame budgetEvery frame fits about 27,000 CPU cycles; the busiest 2-player frame measured is about 23,600
Tested134 automated checks play through every cave, boss and power-up in an emulator
